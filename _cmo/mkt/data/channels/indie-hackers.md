# Indie Hackers Channel Guide

## Overview

Indie Hackers is the most conversion-efficient community for bootstrapped founders in 2026. According to a study of 387 product launches and 156 founders (OpenHunts, 2024), Indie Hackers delivers a **23.1% conversion rate per engaged post** compared to Product Hunt's 3.1% per launch. The audience is small but intensely qualified — these are people who build, buy, and recommend software tools.

Indie Hackers is NOT a launch platform. It is a community-building strategy that requires 4-6 months of sustained engagement. The founders who win here are the ones who show up consistently, share real numbers, and engage with others' journeys. The community is built on reciprocity.

Key insight: Even $0 MRR posts get strong engagement if they include specific lessons learned. The community respects honesty and would rather see "$0 MRR but learned X, Y, Z" than vague "making progress" updates.

## Best Practices

### Journey Posts With Specific Metrics

The highest-performing content type on Indie Hackers is journey posts with real numbers. Revenue figures dramatically boost engagement — posts featuring specific MRR consistently generate 30-50+ comments, while vague "making progress" updates receive minimal traction.

**What to include in every update:**
- Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR) — even if it is $0
- User count and growth rate
- Churn rate and what you are doing about it
- What specifically drove growth or decline this month
- One concrete lesson learned

**Example of a high-performing post title:**
"From $0 to $1K MRR in 8 months — here's the exact breakdown of what worked"

**Example of a low-performing post title:**
"Quick update on my SaaS journey"

### The Milestone System

Indie Hackers has a dedicated milestones feature and leaderboard that gives product page posts significant visibility. Use it.

**Key milestones to post (each is a content opportunity):**
- First commit / first line of code
- Landing page live
- First signup
- First paying customer
- $100 MRR
- $500 MRR
- $1,000 MRR
- $5,000 MRR
- First churn (yes, post about this too)
- First feature request from a paying customer
- First month of profitability

Each milestone post should include: what happened, how long it took, what you did to get there, and what you learned.

### Community Engagement (Non-Negotiable)

Building in public only works if you engage with the community. Posting daily updates without engaging is like talking to an empty room. The Indie Hackers community is built on reciprocity — supporting others means they will support you.

**Engagement rules:**
- For every post you publish, comment thoughtfully on at least 5 other posts
- Celebrate other founders' milestones genuinely
- Answer questions where you have relevant experience
- Share resources and tools you have found useful (not just your own product)
- Join group discussions and AMAs

### Cross-Promotion With Other Builders

Collaborating with other indie hackers at your stage is a force multiplier:
- Guest posts or interviews about each other's building journeys
- Sharing each other's launches across your respective audiences
- Joint webinars or Twitter Spaces about shared challenges
- When you support other builders publicly, their followers discover you

## Content Formats

### Ranked by Engagement on Indie Hackers

1. **Revenue milestone posts** — "$X MRR" posts with detailed breakdowns are the #1 content type. Always include the "how" not just the "what."

2. **Journey posts with metrics** — Monthly or quarterly updates with real numbers. Consistency here builds a following that checks back for your updates.

3. **Post-mortems and failure analysis** — "Why my product failed" or "The feature that almost killed our growth" posts are rare on IH and always get strong engagement.

4. **How-I-Did-It breakdowns** — Step-by-step posts about a specific tactic: "How I got my first 100 users," "My exact cold email sequence that converts at X%."

5. **Ask IH (questions)** — Genuine questions about building, pricing, marketing, or technical decisions. Best when specific: "Should I price at $9 or $19 for [specific product type]?" not "How do I price my SaaS?"

6. **Product launch announcements** — Effective ONLY after you have established presence. Cold launches from unknown accounts get minimal engagement.

7. **Milestones leaderboard posts** — Tied to specific achievements, shown on the milestones page. Lower effort but consistent visibility.

### Content That Falls Flat

- Generic motivational posts ("Just keep going!")
- Posts without specific numbers or details
- Thinly disguised product pitches
- Content that could apply to any product or any founder — the more specific, the better
- Reposts of content from your blog with no IH-specific context

## Timing & Frequency

### Posting Schedule

- **Best days:** Tuesday through Thursday (weekdays during work hours)
- **Best times:** 9 AM - 12 PM ET when the community is most active
- **Avoid:** Weekends (significantly lower engagement), Friday afternoon

### Posting Frequency

- **Journey updates:** 1-2 per month (monthly is the sweet spot for revenue updates)
- **Milestone posts:** Whenever you hit one — do not hold these back
- **Discussion posts / Ask IH:** 1-2 per week maximum
- **Comments on others' posts:** Daily or near-daily (this is where the real relationship building happens)

### The Long Game

Indie Hackers is a 4-6 month investment before you see meaningful returns. The cadence:
- **Month 1-2:** Mostly commenting and engaging. 1-2 original posts to introduce yourself and your project.
- **Month 2-4:** Regular journey updates. Establish your posting rhythm. Start recognizing and being recognized by other regulars.
- **Month 4-6:** Your posts start getting meaningful engagement. Community members actively follow your journey. Milestone posts drive traffic.
- **Month 6+:** Compound returns. You have an audience that anticipates your updates and shares them.

## Rules & Anti-Patterns

### What the Community Penalizes

- **Hit-and-run posting:** Publishing a post and never responding to comments. The community notices.
- **Inflated or vague metrics:** Saying "growing fast" instead of "grew from 50 to 85 users this month." Specificity is currency on IH.
- **Pure self-promotion without story:** "Check out my product [link]" with no context, journey, or lesson. This is not what IH is for.
- **Engagement farming:** Posts designed purely to generate comments ("What's your tech stack?") without genuine interest in the answers.
- **Copying successful post formats without substance:** If your "From $0 to $X" post does not have real detail about the journey, it will feel hollow and the community will call it out.

### What Gets Reported or Flagged

- Spam or repeated promotional posts
- Misleading metrics (inflating numbers, counting free trials as "customers")
- Hostile or unconstructive comments
- Affiliate link spam

### The Authenticity Test

Before posting, ask: "If another founder posted this, would I find it genuinely useful or interesting?" If the answer is no, revise or do not post.

## Strategy Integration

### When to Recommend This Channel

- **Best for:** Bootstrapped SaaS, developer tools, productivity tools, any product built by a solo founder or small team
- **Stage:** Idea validation through early growth. Most effective from pre-launch through $10K MRR.
- **Effort level:** MEDIUM — 3-5 hours/week (commenting + monthly posts)
- **Time to results:** 4-6 months for meaningful community presence, but early milestone posts can drive traffic sooner
- **Impact potential:** HIGH for conversion quality (23.1% conversion per engaged post), MEDIUM for volume

### Why 23.1% Conversion Matters

The Indie Hackers audience is pre-qualified. They are founders, builders, and early adopters who:
- Understand the value of software tools
- Are willing to pay for products that solve real problems
- Try new products as part of their professional routine
- Provide quality feedback that improves your product

This means 100 engaged readers from Indie Hackers may be worth more than 10,000 from a general traffic source.

### Funnel Position

- **Top of funnel:** Journey posts and milestone updates attract followers
- **Middle of funnel:** Consistent, transparent updates build trust over months
- **Bottom of funnel:** Product page, milestone posts with links, and direct conversations in comments

### Synergies with Other Channels

- IH milestone posts become build-in-public Twitter/X threads
- IH journey posts provide material for blog content and SEO
- Community feedback becomes product development input
- Relationships with other IH founders create cross-promotion opportunities
- IH success metrics can be referenced in Hacker News Show HN posts ("validated with 200 beta users from the IH community")

## Cold Start: Zero Followers Playbook

### Week 1: Setup

1. **Create your profile and product page:**
   - Use your real name and photo
   - Write a clear, honest one-line description of what you are building
   - Set up your product page even if the product is not live yet — you can list it as "in development"
   - Add your tech stack, tagline, and a link to your landing page or waitlist

2. **Read the top 20 posts from the past month.** Note the format, tone, and specificity level that gets engagement. Note what gets ignored.

### Week 1-4: Engagement Phase

3. **Comment on 3-5 posts per day.** Prioritize:
   - Journey posts from founders at a similar stage to yours
   - "Ask IH" posts where you have relevant experience
   - Milestone celebration posts (genuine congratulations + relevant follow-up question)

4. **Be helpful, not promotional.** Share your experiences, tools you use, approaches that worked for you. Do NOT mention your product in comments unless directly asked.

5. **Start recognizing regulars.** The IH community has a core group of active members. Engage with their content consistently — they will notice and reciprocate.

### Month 1-2: First Posts

6. **Introductory post:** "I'm building [product] to solve [problem]. Here's why, and here's where I am."
   - Include your background (briefly)
   - The problem you experienced personally
   - Your approach and current progress
   - Specific questions for the community
   - End with: "I'll be sharing monthly updates with real numbers."

7. **First milestone post** — even if the milestone is small. "Just got my first 10 signups — here's what I learned about my landing page." Small milestones with genuine insights always outperform big milestones with no detail.

### Month 2-4: Rhythm

8. **Monthly revenue/progress updates.** Same format every month so followers know what to expect:
   - Last month: [MRR/users/key metric]
   - This month: [MRR/users/key metric]
   - What changed and why
   - Biggest lesson
   - Next month's focus

9. **One "How I Did X" post per month** breaking down a specific tactic or decision. These get bookmarked and shared.

10. **Continue engaging daily.** The ratio should remain roughly 5:1 (comments to posts).

### Month 4+: Compounding

11. Your consistent presence starts paying off. Community members reference your journey, recommend your product in relevant threads, and actively follow your updates.

## Budget Section

### $0 Budget Strategy (This Is the Default)

Indie Hackers is fundamentally a $0 channel. The entire strategy above requires zero spend:
- Posting is free
- Product pages are free
- Milestones are free
- Community engagement is free

The "cost" is your time — 3-5 hours per week of genuine participation.

### $0 with Email Integration

The highest-value free addition: include a link to your email newsletter/waitlist in your IH profile and product page. Every journey post reader who clicks through is a warm lead. This costs nothing except setting up an email capture page.

### With Budget ($50-200/month)

Indie Hackers does not have a traditional ad platform. Budget allocation for IH-adjacent activities:

- **Email tool (e.g., Buttondown, free tier):** Capture leads from IH traffic with a landing page
- **Analytics tool:** Track which IH posts drive the most conversions to understand what content to double down on
- **Cross-promotion budget:** If collaborating with another IH founder on a joint launch, small budget for shared landing page or design work

### What NOT to Spend On

- Paying for upvotes or engagement (violates community trust)
- Hiring someone to post on your behalf (the community will notice the voice change)
- Running ads that point to Indie Hackers posts (ineffective, the traffic needs to be organic community members)

## Content Module Integration

### Hook Patterns That Work on Indie Hackers

The post title is your hook. IH titles should be specific, metric-driven, and honest.

- **Revenue milestone:** "From $0 to $4K MRR in 2 months — exact breakdown of every acquisition channel"
- **Failure/lesson:** "$8K spent on ads, 0 customers. Switching to content marketing. Here's the plan."
- **Specific tactic:** "The one cold email change that took my reply rate from 3% to 18%"
- **Journey update:** "Month 6 update: $2.1K MRR, 43 customers, and the feature I almost didn't build"
- **Honest question:** "My churn hit 15% this month. Those of you who've fixed high churn — what actually worked?"

### Post Structure Template: Monthly Update

```
# [Month] Update: [Key Metric Headline]

## Numbers
- MRR: $X (up/down from $Y)
- Customers: X (net new: +/-Y)
- Churn: X%
- Traffic: X visitors from [sources]

## What Happened
[2-3 paragraphs on the key events, decisions, and outcomes this month]

## What Worked
[Specific tactic or decision that drove growth, with enough detail that someone else could replicate it]

## What Didn't Work
[Something you tried that failed — and why you think it failed]

## Lesson of the Month
[One concrete takeaway]

## Next Month's Focus
[Your #1 priority and why]

## Questions for the Community
[1-2 specific questions where you genuinely want input]
```

### Post Structure Template: Milestone Post

```
# [Milestone]: Here's How I Got Here

## The Milestone
[What you achieved and when]

## The Timeline
[How long it took from start to this point]

## The Breakdown
[Exactly what led to this milestone — channels, tactics, decisions]

## What I'd Do Differently
[Honest reflection on mistakes or inefficiencies]

## What's Next
[Your next target milestone and plan to get there]
```

### Voice Notes for Indie Hackers

- **Tone:** Honest, specific, peer-to-peer. You are talking to other founders who are in the trenches.
- **Never sound like a press release.** "We are thrilled to announce" is the kiss of death.
- **Be vulnerable about failures.** The community rewards honesty about mistakes more than bragging about wins.
- **Use specific numbers everywhere.** Not "growing nicely" but "grew from 23 to 41 paying customers." Not "improved conversion" but "landing page conversion went from 2.1% to 3.8% after changing the headline."
- **Acknowledge what you do not know.** "I'm not sure if this is the right approach — has anyone tried X?" is more engaging than pretending you have all the answers.
- **Good example:** "Hit $500 MRR this week. Took 5 months. Majority of growth came from 3 Reddit comments (not posts — comments) where I helped people with problems my tool solves. Zero ad spend. Biggest mistake was spending 2 months on a feature only 1 person asked for."
- **Bad example:** "Excited to share that our innovative SaaS platform has reached a significant revenue milestone! We're transforming the way teams work."
