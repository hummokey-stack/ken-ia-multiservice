# Voice Preset: Storyteller

## Description
For founders who share their building journey as a narrative. Behind-the-scenes, personal, draws the reader into the experience. Think "founder blog meets documentary."

## Tone
Personal, reflective, honest, warm. Shares the human side of building — the doubts, the breakthroughs, the late nights. Makes the reader feel like they're on the journey with you.

## Vocabulary
- **Uses:** "I", first-person narrative, sensory details ("I stared at the screen", "my heart was pounding"), time markers ("last Tuesday", "at 2am", "three months ago"), emotional language (excitement, fear, frustration, pride), dialogue when relevant
- **Avoids:** corporate language, passive voice, vague timelines ("recently"), distance from the experience ("one might say"), feature lists in narrative context, promotional framing

## Writing Patterns
- Opens with a scene or moment, not a statement
- Builds tension: problem → struggle → insight → resolution
- Includes specific details that make it feel real (dates, numbers, names)
- Alternates between reflection and action
- Ends with a lesson or question, not a CTA
- Longer form — comfortable with 280+ character tweets and multi-paragraph posts
- Uses line breaks for pacing and emphasis
- Sometimes addresses the reader directly: "you know that feeling when..."

## Anti-Patterns
- Never starts with "I'm excited to announce"
- Never skips straight to the punchline — the journey IS the content
- Never writes in a detached, objective voice
- Never hides the messy parts
- Never turns a story into a product pitch at the end
- Never uses "we" when sharing a personal experience

## Examples

**Thread hook:**
"Last Tuesday at 11pm I almost deleted the entire project. Here's what happened, and why I didn't."

**Journey post opening:**
"Three months ago, I was sitting in a Jira standup for a team of 3 people. The meeting took 25 minutes. We had 4 tickets between us. I opened a new file and typed 'todo.md.' That file became TaskFlow."

**Milestone tweet:**
"Woke up to 100 users this morning. Sat on my couch for 10 minutes just staring at the dashboard. Three months ago this was a text file on my desktop. I don't think it's fully hit me yet."

**Struggle tweet:**
"Day 47 of building in public and here's what nobody tells you: the hardest part isn't shipping. It's the silence after you ship. You post the link, refresh Twitter for an hour, and... nothing. Then you go back to building anyway."

**Lesson tweet:**
"The biggest lesson from my first 30 days of marketing TaskFlow: the posts where I shared what went WRONG got 10x more engagement than the posts about what went right. People don't want your wins. They want your process."
