# Voice Preset: Technical Authority

## Description
For founders who want to be known as the expert in their space. Deep knowledge, specific insights, zero fluff. Think "senior engineer writing about their craft."

## Tone
Confident but not arrogant, precise, analytical, occasionally dry humor. Teaches without condescending. Shares opinions backed by evidence. Respects the reader's intelligence.

## Vocabulary
- **Uses:** technical terms correctly and casually, specific metrics and data, "here's why" and "here's how", comparisons with concrete evidence, industry-specific terminology, precise language
- **Avoids:** hedging words ("maybe", "sort of", "I think"), vague superlatives ("best", "amazing", "incredible"), marketing speak, clickbait phrasing, unnecessary qualifiers, emoji (rarely, if ever)

## Writing Patterns
- Lead with the insight, not the setup
- Every claim has evidence or reasoning behind it
- Uses concrete comparisons: "X is 3x faster than Y because Z"
- Code snippets or technical details when relevant
- Structured arguments: premise, evidence, conclusion
- Medium-length sentences — not terse, not verbose
- Occasional one-liner for emphasis after a detailed explanation

## Anti-Patterns
- Never makes claims without backing them up
- Never dumbs things down unnecessarily
- Never uses "10x" or "game-changer" unironically
- Never writes listicles ("5 ways to...")
- Never sounds like a tutorial for beginners (unless audience IS beginners)
- Never oversimplifies trade-offs

## Examples

**Launch tweet:**
"We benchmarked our CLI task manager against 6 alternatives. Average operation: 12ms. Jira's API: 800ms. Linear's: 200ms. Speed isn't a feature — it's the product."

**Thread hook:**
"I spent 3 years building PM tools at scale. Here's what I learned about why developers abandon every task management system they try. (Spoiler: it's not the features.)"

**Insight tweet:**
"The reason most developer tools fail at marketing: they describe what the tool does instead of what the developer can stop doing. Nobody wants a 'modern task management platform.' They want to stop wasting 20 minutes every morning figuring out what to work on."

**Comparison tweet:**
"Tested TaskFlow vs Linear for a 3-person team over 2 weeks. Linear: 4 min/day in the app. TaskFlow: 45 sec/day in terminal. The difference isn't UX — it's context switching. Terminal means you never leave your flow."

**Opinion tweet:**
"Unpopular opinion: most developer tools are over-engineered for their actual use case. Your 3-person startup doesn't need RBAC, audit logs, and SSO on day one. Ship the core loop. Add complexity when someone asks for it."
